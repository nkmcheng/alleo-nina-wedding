Guest Dress Code — Garden Dressy Casual

03 · For the Invitation

Polished, comfortable, wedding-appropriate — not formal. A little nicer than a nice lunch out or a family celebration. With 200–250 guests of every age, the goal is that nobody feels they must buy something new: no suits, blazers, ties, gowns, or floor-length dresses required. Deliberately not called "semi-formal", which reads dressier than intended.

Ladies

A dress is lovely but never required. Midi, maxi, knee- or tea-length dresses · jumpsuits · satin or dressy blouse with trousers or a skirt · coordinated separates · anything polished already in the closet. Shoes: flats, sandals, low heels, clean sneakers.

Gentlemen

No coat, tie, or suit. Polo or knit polo with chinos/trousers · short-sleeve, linen, or button-down shirt (sleeves rolled up is fine) · dress shirt and trousers · barong, optional. Shoes: loafers, dress shoes, clean sneakers.

The sweet spot

  • More than you need: suits, blazers, ties, tuxedos, formal floor-length gowns, anything bought just for the day
  • Just right: flowy midi dress + sandals · satin blouse, trousers + flats · polo, chinos + loafers · linen shirt + trousers
  • A little too relaxed: plain tees, tank tops, jerseys, gym wear, joggers, shorts, beachwear, flip-flops, ripped jeans, house clothes
  • Clean, dark, well-styled jeans are okay; trousers or chinos preferred

Flowers & Tables

04 · Ceremony & Reception Styling

The February advantage — and one warning

Early February is peak dry season: Baguio-supplied blooms are abundant and Tagaytay is at its coolest. But the date is one week before Valentine's, when flower prices surge — book the florist early and lock a priced flower list, with quantities and varieties fixed regardless of Valentine's market prices.

The teal floral recipe

  • Ivory heroes: ivory roses, casablanca lilies (fragrant — for the chapel), white chrysanthemums, baby's breath, white carnations
  • Blue accents: pale blue hydrangea, misty blue limonium, steel-blue eryngium thistle
  • Greenery: silver-dollar eucalyptus, italian ruscus, ferns
  • The teal itself: true teal blooms don't exist — the color lives in ribbons, runners, napkins, candles, and stationery

Chapel on the Hill — keep it light

  • An octagonal garden chapel — terracotta roof, open verandas, brick path — it barely needs décor
  • Baby's-breath + eucalyptus clusters on aisle stands
  • Two ivory arrangements flanking the altar; ivory and pale-blue petals for the flower girls
  • Skip drapery and arches — save the budget for the reception

Hillcreek reception — simple, as planned

  • No ceiling treatment, no trusses — the Grand Ballroom's chandeliers and arched windows carry the room
  • Linens: ivory base cloths with ocean-teal runners and napkins, gold-rimmed plates and votives
  • Centerpieces: low ivory arrangements with hydrangea and eucalyptus, 2–3 candle votives — kept low for a lunch crowd
  • Backdrop: white panels with ivory blooms, greenery, and teal ribbon behind the couple's table; warm-white uplighting from Artuz (no colored uplights against the walls)
  • Hero tables: spend on the couple's table, cake table, and entrance arrangement; guest tables go carnation-and-mum heavy — at 25 tables the savings are real

Cocktail hour — the garden lawn

On Hillcreek's balustraded sunken lawn while the couple takes portraits, ringed by the booths: Massage Pop-Up Lounge · Coffee & Matcha Bar · Street Food & Kakanin · Donut Wall · Tequila Shot Wall · Shakes — Spiked & Sweet · Dimsum Bar · Fortune Cookie Wall · DIY Brick Keychains · Photo Booth & Prints · Pizza Corner. Cocktail tables in ivory with teal runners; string lights between the trees. Acsions band plays the wedding march and reception sets.

Who receives what

  • Entourage — proposal card (Card 4) as early as possible, so fittings can start; their names later appear on the main invitation's entourage insert. No RSVP needed — a yes to Card 4 is the RSVP.
  • Principal sponsors — Card 2, sent before general invitations; their names traditionally print on the main invitation.
  • Secondary sponsors — Card 3, alongside the principal sponsors' wave.
  • Guests — main invitation + Card 1 + their personal website link (auto-filled name and reserved seats).
