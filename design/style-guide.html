Guest Dress Code — Garden Dressy Casual

03 · For the Invitation

Polished, comfortable, wedding-appropriate — not formal. A little nicer than a nice lunch out or a family celebration. With 200–250 guests of every age, the goal is that nobody feels they must buy something new: no suits, blazers, ties, gowns, or floor-length dresses required. Deliberately not called "semi-formal", which reads dressier than intended.

Ladies

A dress is lovely but never required. Midi, maxi, knee- or tea-length dresses · jumpsuits · satin or dressy blouse with trousers or a skirt · coordinated separates · anything polished already in the closet. Shoes: flats, sandals, low heels, clean sneakers.

Gentlemen

No coat, tie, or suit. Polo or knit polo with chinos/trousers · short-sleeve, linen, or button-down shirt (sleeves rolled up is fine) · dress shirt and trousers · barong, optional. Shoes: loafers, dress shoes, clean sneakers.

The sweet spot

  • More than you need: suits, blazers, ties, tuxedos, formal floor-length gowns, anything bought just for the day
  • Just right: flowy midi dress + sandals · satin blouse, trousers + flats · polo, chinos + loafers · linen shirt + trousers
  • A little too relaxed: plain tees, tank tops, jerseys, gym wear, joggers, shorts, beachwear, flip-flops, ripped jeans, house clothes
  • Clean, dark, well-styled jeans are okay; trousers or chinos preferred

Flowers & Tables

04 · Ceremony & Reception Styling

The February advantage — and one warning

Early February is peak dry season: Baguio-supplied blooms are abundant and Tagaytay is at its coolest. But the date is one week before Valentine's, when flower prices surge — the florist is booked — keep the priced flower list locked, with quantities and varieties fixed regardless of Valentine's market prices.

The teal floral recipe

  • Ivory heroes: ivory roses, casablanca lilies (fragrant — for the chapel), white chrysanthemums, baby's breath, white carnations
  • Blue accents: pale blue hydrangea, misty blue limonium, steel-blue eryngium thistle
  • Greenery: silver-dollar eucalyptus, italian ruscus, ferns
  • The teal itself: true teal blooms don't exist — the color lives in ribbons, runners, napkins, candles, and stationery

Every flower, with pictures, is in the Flower Guide below.

Chapel on the Hill — keep it light

  • An octagonal garden chapel — terracotta roof, open verandas, brick path — it barely needs décor
  • Baby's-breath + eucalyptus clusters on aisle stands
  • Two ivory arrangements flanking the altar; ivory and pale-blue petals for the flower girls
  • Skip drapery and arches — save the budget for the reception

Hillcreek reception — simple, as planned

  • No ceiling treatment, no trusses — the Grand Ballroom's chandeliers and arched windows carry the room
  • Linens: ivory base cloths with ocean-teal runners and napkins, gold-rimmed plates and votives
  • Centerpieces: low ivory arrangements with hydrangea and eucalyptus, 2–3 candle votives — kept low for a lunch crowd
  • Backdrop: white panels with ivory blooms, greenery, and teal ribbon behind the couple's table; warm-white uplighting from Artuz (no colored uplights against the walls)
  • Hero tables: spend on the couple's table, cake table, and entrance arrangement; guest tables go carnation-and-mum heavy — at 25 tables the savings are real

Cocktail hour — the garden lawn

On Hillcreek's balustraded sunken lawn while the couple takes portraits, ringed by the booths: Massage Pop-Up Lounge · Coffee & Matcha Bar · Street Food & Kakanin · Donut Wall · Tequila Shot Wall · Shakes — Spiked & Sweet · Dimsum Bar · Fortune Cookie Wall · DIY Brick Keychains · Photo Booth & Prints (booked) · Pizza Corner. Cocktail tables in ivory with teal runners; string lights between the trees. Acsions band plays the wedding march and reception sets.

Stylist Mood Boards

04b · For the Church & Reception Stylists

One board per stylist. Send the image together with its brief below. The boards set the mood; the briefs are what to actually build.

Chapel on the Hill — for the church stylist

Brief

  • Colors: white and ivory flowers with soft green foliage only. No colored blooms, no blue accents.
  • Entrance: a pair of aged-terracotta urns of white roses, hydrangea, and trailing eucalyptus on either side of the brick path, at the chapel doors.
  • Aisle: loose clusters of baby's breath and silver-dollar eucalyptus on the pew ends, tied with thin ivory silk ribbon; ivory rose petals down the center.
  • Altar: two generous white arrangements flanking it — roses, casablanca lilies (fragrant), white chrysanthemums, baby's breath, ferns, eucalyptus. Nothing else on the altar.
  • Small touches: one or two low white arrangements on side tables or window ledges.
  • Please skip: drapery, arches, floor candles, signage, and anything dyed. The terracotta roof, columns, and brick path are the décor.

Hillcreek Gardens — for the reception stylist

Brief

  • Mood: light, airy, organic, asymmetric. Not tropical, not rustic, no tight floral balls.
  • Colors: about 65–70% ivory, cream, and warm-white flowers · 20–25% soft greenery · 5–10% dusty-blue accents · a touch of champagne. Teal lives in linens and ribbons, never in the flowers.
  • Couple backdrop: gets the strongest floral budget — ivory and white blooms with greenery and dusty-blue accents.
  • Tables: ivory linens, ocean-teal runners and napkins, gold-rimmed chargers, gold votives.
  • Foyer: mostly ivory and green — the mural already brings the blue-teal.
  • Garden booths (cocktail hour): light, matching ivory-and-green accents only.
  • Room: no ceiling treatment or draping; warm-white light only, no colored uplighting.

Please avoid

  • Bright turquoise or royal-blue flowers
  • Strong yellow, orange, hot pink, red, burgundy, or purple-heavy mixes
  • Tropical leaves like monstera or palm as the main foliage
  • Heavy dark-green installations
  • Neon or artificial-looking dyed flowers

To confirm with the stylist

  • Centerpieces: the board suggests a mix — 50% low, 30% medium, 20% tall; the earlier plan was all low for a lunch crowd. Pick one.
  • Backdrop: the board shows a floor-level setup with a couch and an L·N monogram; the earlier plan was a head table in front of white panels. Pick one.
  • The board's "Chapel Florals" panel is a placeholder — use the chapel board above instead.

Flower Guide

04c · Every bloom in the plan

Every flower and green in the plan, what it looks like, and where it goes. The rule of thumb: the chapel is all white and green; the reception is mostly ivory and green with a little dusty blue. Teal never appears in the flowers — only in the linens and ribbons.

The heroes — the big, show-stopping blooms

Ivory roses

Chapel · Reception

Classic creamy-white roses — the backbone of everything.

Use in every arrangement. Book early: roses spike in price the week before Valentine’s.

Garden roses

Reception

Cupped, many-petaled, romantic roses in cream and blush.

A splurge, often imported. Save for the couple backdrop and the bridal bouquet.

Casablanca lilies

Chapel

Large white trumpet lilies with a strong, sweet scent.

Perfect for the open-air chapel altar. Pull the orange pollen tips off so they can’t stain gowns.

White hydrangea

Chapel · Reception

Big, full, cloud-like flower heads.

Fills space generously. Thirsty — keep in water until the last moment.

White lisianthus

Reception

Soft, ruffled, rose-like blooms on slim stems.

Adds lightness between the roses and lasts well.

White spray roses

Many small roses on each stem.

Great in bouquets and centerpieces to fill gaps.

White stock

Reception

Tall columns of ruffled little blooms, lightly scented.

Gives height to medium and tall pieces.

White chrysanthemums

Chapel · Reception

Full, round, cushiony blooms.

Long-lasting and budget-friendly bulk.

White carnations

Reception

Frilly, sturdy, classic.

Classic lacy fern fronds.

Where each goes

  • Chapel entrance & altar: ivory roses, casablanca lilies, white hydrangea, white mums, spray roses, eucalyptus, leatherleaf ferns
  • Chapel aisle: baby’s breath and eucalyptus clusters; ivory rose petals
  • Couple backdrop: garden roses, ivory roses, white and dusty-blue hydrangea, lisianthus, delphinium, trailing ivy (orchids optional)
  • Centerpieces: ivory roses, white and dusty-blue hydrangea, spray roses, lisianthus, stock, eucalyptus, ruscus
  • Guest tables on a budget: carnations and mums as the bulk, with a few roses and a hydrangea head
  • Cake table: a few ivory roses or orchids, trailing ivy

Bouquets & boutonnières

  • Bride: ivory and garden roses, lisianthus, spray roses, a touch of pale-blue hydrangea, eucalyptus; teal ribbon
  • Maid of honor: like the bridesmaids’, slightly fuller
  • Bridesmaids: ivory roses and eucalyptus, tied with teal satin ribbon
  • Groom & groomsmen: eucalyptus with one small blue eryngium or pale-blue bloom
  • Flower girls: ivory and pale-blue petals

Buying tip: the wedding is one week before Valentine’s. Lock a priced flower list with the florist early. Baguio-grown roses, mums, carnations, and baby’s breath are the most reliable; garden roses, delphinium, and eryngium are often imported.

The RSVP Suite

05 · Digital + Print

RSVPs run through the wedding website: every party receives a personal invitation link that opens the RSVP card at the bottom of the main page, greets them by name, and shows their reserved seats. Replies land in the couple's Google Sheet. The printed cards below are the companion for guests who prefer paper.

How the online RSVP works

  • The sheet: "Alleo & Nina - RSVP" in nkmcheng@gmail.com's Google Drive. One row per invitation: Name, Role, Additional guests, Contact. The RSVP menu makes each row's private code and link.
  • Seats: reserved = 1 + additional guests. Guests may bring fewer, or ask for up to 2 more. Extras are only a request, flagged in "Extra seats requested" for the couple to approve.
  • Names: one name line per person attending, the first prefilled with the invitee.
  • Notes & requests: optional box for dietary needs, questions, or requests like "can I bring one more?"
  • Changes: replying again updates the same row; the Log tab keeps every submission.
  • Privacy: guest names live only in the sheet, never in the website's public code.

Setup and day-to-day use: rsvp-sheet/SETUP.md. The sheet's script: rsvp-sheet/Code.gs.

The card changes with the Role

  • Principal Sponsor: "Thank you for standing with us as one of our principal sponsors." · links to their attire colors
  • Secondary Sponsor: "Thank you for taking part in our ceremony." · attire colors
  • Entourage: "We can't wait to have you beside us." · attire colors
  • Family: "It wouldn't be the same without you." · what to wear
  • Guest: no label or line · what to wear

Everyone except plain guests also sees their role in small gold capitals under their name.
